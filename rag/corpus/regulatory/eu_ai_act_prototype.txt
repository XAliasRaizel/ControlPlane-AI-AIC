EU AI Act — Prototype Regulatory Corpus (Summary Form)
========================================================
NOTE: Plain-language summary for Policy RAG demo/retrieval purposes, not
verbatim legal text of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. Verified against current
public reporting as of August 2026; confirm against the official Official
Journal text and any further Digital Omnibus amendments before real use --
this regulation's timeline has already changed once (see below) and may
change again.

Overview and phased timeline
----------------------------------------------------------------
The EU AI Act entered into force on 1 August 2024 and applies obligations
in stages rather than all at once:
  - 2 February 2025: prohibited AI practices (e.g. certain social scoring,
    manipulative or exploitative systems, some biometric categorisation)
    became enforceable.
  - 2 August 2025: obligations for providers of general-purpose AI (GPAI)
    models took effect.
  - 2 August 2026: Article 50 transparency obligations became enforceable
    -- disclosure that a user is interacting with an AI system, labelling
    of synthetic/deepfake content, and marking of AI-generated content.
    This date has already passed as of this document's writing and these
    obligations are now in effect.
  - High-risk AI system obligations (Annex III: biometrics, critical
    infrastructure, education, employment, essential services, law
    enforcement, migration, and administration of justice) were originally
    also scheduled for 2 August 2026. A "Digital Omnibus on AI," reaching
    political agreement in mid-2026, deferred the Annex III high-risk
    obligations to 2 December 2027 (Annex I product-embedded high-risk
    systems remain on a later 2 August 2028 track). Treat this deferral as
    provisional pending final confirmation in the Official Journal.

Practical implication for an internal governance tool
----------------------------------------------------------------
Two distinct obligation tracks matter for a system like ControlPlane:
  1. Transparency (Article 50) -- already in force. Any user-facing AI
     interaction (e.g. the HR/support chatbot) should be clearly
     disclosed as AI-driven, and any synthetic content it produces that
     could be mistaken for authentic material should be identifiable as
     AI-generated.
  2. High-risk system obligations (Annex III) -- deferred, but still
     coming. Employment decisions and access to essential services are
     explicitly listed high-risk categories. This is the same reasoning
     ControlPlane already applies internally by tagging loan-decision and
     hiring-decision applications as critical/high-impact and routing
     them toward human review -- that design choice anticipates the
     Annex III obligations for risk management, human oversight, logging,
     and record-keeping, even though the hard legal deadline for those
     specific obligations has moved out to December 2027.

What this corpus intentionally does not cover
----------------------------------------------------------------
This summary does not cover conformity assessment procedures, CE marking,
EU database registration, or the specific technical documentation format
required under Annex IV -- those are compliance-process requirements for
providers placing high-risk systems on the market, not directly relevant
to an internal governance control plane's retrieval corpus.
